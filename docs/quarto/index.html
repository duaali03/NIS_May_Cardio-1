Association of Atrial Fibrillation with In-Hospital Outcomes Among Cardiac Surgery Hospitalizations: An analysis of the national inpatient sample (NIS) database

Analysis for NIS Cardio-1

Author
Affiliation

Dua Ali

Dow University of Health Sciences

Preamble:

  • Reference Papers:

  • Salman et al. 2025

  • Rao et al. 2024

Study Objective: To compare in-hospital outcomes and complications between cardiac surgery patients with and without atrial fibrillation or flutter in U.S. hospitals, adjusting for demographic, socioeconomic, clinical, and hospital-related factors.

Data Source: Cross-sectional analysis using the National Inpatient Sample (NIS) database, a representative sample of all-payer inpatient hospitalizations in the U.S, from 2017 to 2022.

Patient Selection: Included all inpatient admissions for patients 18 years or older who underwent cardiac surgery, identified using ICD-10-PCS procedure codes. Index cardiac surgery was classified into two subtypes: Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting (0210x–0213x) and Valvular Surgery (02QF-Jx, 02RF-Jx), identified using ICD-10-PCS procedure codes. From this cohort, patients were categorized into two groups based on the presence or absence of atrial fibrillation or flutter, identified using ICD-10-CM diagnosis codes (I48.x).

Outcomes of Interest:

  • In-hospital Mortality

  • In-hospital Stroke

  • Length of Stay (days)

  • Inflation-adjusted Total Charge ($) [adjusted to 2022 dollars, using CPI data by the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics]

Statistical Analysis: Univariable and multivariable analyses were conducted to evaluate the association between atrial fibrillation or flutter and inpatient outcomes among cardiac surgery hospitalizations:

  • Univariable Analysis:

    • Continuous variables: Design-based Kruskal-Wallis test.

    • Categorical variables: Pearson’s X² test with Rao & Scott adjustment to account for survey design.

  • Multivariable Analysis:

    • Logistic regression for binary outcomes (e.g., in-hospital mortality, in-hospital stroke).

    • Linear regression for continuous outcomes (e.g., length of stay, total charges).

  • Adjustments: Models controlled for the following covariates:

    • Demographics and Socioeconomic Factors: Age, Sex, Race, Residential income, Expected primary payer.

    • Clinical Factors: Index cardiac surgery type (CABG vs. Valvular), Hyperlipidemia, Obesity, Diabetes Mellitus, Chronic Kidney Disease, Current Smoking, Hypertension, Heart failure, History of Stroke or TIA .

    • Hospital-Level Factors: Hospital region, Hospital bedsize, Hospital location and teaching status.

  • Software: All analyses were performed using R Statistical Language (Version 4.5.0; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria), incorporating survey-weighted procedures via the survey package to account for the complex sampling design of NIS.
