In-hospital Outcomes and Complications of Patients with Atrial Fibrillation After Cardiac Surgery: An analysis of the national inpatient sample (NIS) database

Analysis for NIS Cardio-1

Author
Affiliation

Dua Ali

Dow University of Health Sciences

Preamble:

  • Reference Papers:

  • Salman et al. 2025

  • Rao et al. 2024

Study Objective: To compare in-hospital outcomes and complications between cardiac surgery patients with and without atrial fibrillation or flutter in U.S. hospitals, adjusting for demographic, socioeconomic, clinical, and hospital-related factors.

Data Source: Cross-sectional analysis using the National Inpatient Sample (NIS) database, a representative sample of all-payer inpatient hospitalizations in the U.S.

Patient Selection: Included all inpatient admissions for patients 18 years or older who underwent cardiac surgery, identified using ICD-10-PCS procedure codes. Index cardiac surgery was classified into two subtypes: Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting (CABG) and Valvular Surgery. From this cohort, patients were categorized into two groups based on the presence or absence of atrial fibrillation or flutter, identified using ICD-10-CM diagnosis codes (I48.x).

Outcomes of Interest:

  • In-hospital Mortality

  • In-hospital Stroke

  • Length of Stay (days)

  • Inflation-adjusted Total Charge ($) [adjusted to 2022 dollars, using CPI data by the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics]

Statistical Analysis: Univariable and multivariable analyses were conducted to evaluate the association between atrial fibrillation or flutter and inpatient outcomes among cardiac surgery hospitalizations:

  • Univariable Analysis:

    • Continuous variables: Design-based Kruskal-Wallis test.

    • Categorical variables: Pearson’s X² test with Rao & Scott adjustment to account for survey design.

  • Multivariable Analysis:

    • Logistic regression for binary outcomes (e.g., in-hospital mortality, Stroke).

    • Linear regression for continuous outcomes (e.g., length of stay, total charges).

  • Adjustments: Models controlled for the following covariates:

    • Demographics and Socioeconomic Factors: Age, Sex, Race, Residential income, Expected primary payer.

    • Clinical Factors: Elixhauser Comorbidity Index, Index cardiac surgery type (CABG vs. Valvular), Hyperlipidemia, Current Smoking, History of Stroke or TIA, History of Heart Failure, Previous Myocardial Infarction.

    • Hospital-Level Factors: Hospital region, Hospital bedsize, Hospital location and teaching status.

  • Software: All statistical analyses were performed using R Statistical Language (Version 4.5.0; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria), incorporating survey-weighted procedures via the survey package to account for the complex sampling design of NIS.
